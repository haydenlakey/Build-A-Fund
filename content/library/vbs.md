## What happened

**VBS Mutual Bank** was a small, financially struggling mutual bank in Limpopo whose business was mostly home
loans. In the year to March 2014 it reported a loss of about R1.9 million and held R285 million of depositors'
money (investigator's report).

From 2014 its new leadership changed course. The bank stopped relying on small retail depositors and went after
**very large, short-term deposits from municipalities**, and replaced its conservative lending with "far more ambitious" deals such as fuel and
contract financing, much of it to clients connected to its own leaders. Reported depositors' funds grew from
**R285 million (2014) to R1.55 billion (2017)**, and loans from R210 million to R1.09 billion.

On **11 March 2018**, against the background of a severe liquidity crisis, the Minister of Finance placed VBS
under curatorship. The curator's first findings revealed "massive losses", and the Reserve Bank's Prudential
Authority appointed Advocate Terry Motau SC to investigate. His report, published in October 2018, was titled
**"The Great Bank Heist"**.

## How it worked

The investigator described "two principal pillars of unlawfulness":

- **Looting.** Large payments were made to those who ran the scheme, with "substantial bribes" to directors and
  others "to buy their silence". The bank attracted municipal deposits by paying so-called **"commissions"**,
  often including bribes to officials. Its systems were manipulated to create "enormous fictitious deposits" for
  the controlling company, Vele, and its associates.
- **Concealment.** VBS's audited financial statements for the year to March 2017, and its monthly returns to the
  banking regulator, contained "fraudulent misrepresentations". The KPMG engagement partner on the 2017 audit had
  "very substantial facilities from VBS" that he did not declare to KPMG, and "gave an unqualified audit opinion in
  circumstances where he knew the financial statements were misstated".

The investigator could not establish the precise amount looted, but the forensic accountants found that
**R1 894 923 674 was "gratuitously received" from VBS by some 53 people and companies** between 1 March 2015 and
17 June 2018. Vele and its associates received about R937 million and the bank's chairman about R326 million.

## Who was hurt

Municipalities, and through them the residents whose money they held. VBS received over **R3.4 billion** from
municipalities from January 2015 to the curatorship and paid back just over R2.3 billion; on 30 April 2018,
**R1.23 billion of municipal deposits** was still in the bank. One municipality's acting chief financial officer
noticed that the law governing municipal finances allowed deposits only with banks registered under the Banks Act,
which excluded mutual banks, and resisted pressure until VBS returned her municipality's money (investigator's
report).

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **A deposit is a loan to the bank.** Cash in a bank is only as safe as the bank's own lending and governance.
- **Very fast growth in a small financial firm is a warning sign,** especially when it is paying unusually to
  attract money.
- **An audit opinion is a check, not a guarantee.** VBS's 2017 financial statements carried an unqualified
  (clean) audit opinion from a Big Four firm, though they were fraudulently misstated.
- **Rules about where money may be held exist for a reason.** The one official who followed them got her
  municipality's money out.
