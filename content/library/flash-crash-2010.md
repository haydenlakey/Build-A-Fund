## What happened

6 May 2010 was already a nervous day, with worries about Greece's government debt and a falling euro. By 2:30 p.m.
the VIX volatility index was up 22.5% and US share indices were down over 4% (CFTC–SEC report).

At **2:32 p.m.** a large mutual fund group started selling **75,000 E-Mini S&P 500 futures contracts**, worth about
**US\$4.1 billion**, to hedge its share holdings. It used an automated program set to sell at **9% of the previous
minute's trading volume**, "without regard to price or time". A similar sale the year before had taken more than
five hours; this one took about **20 minutes**.

High-frequency traders bought at first, then sold their positions on, passing contracts back and forth: between
2:45:13 and 2:45:27 they traded over 27,000 contracts while buying only about 200 more. The rising volume made the
sell program sell faster, although, as the report notes, "high trading volume is not necessarily a reliable
indicator of market liquidity". Buy orders waiting in the futures market fell to **less than 1%** of their level that
morning.

From 2:41 p.m. to 2:45:27 p.m. the futures fell more than **5%** and the SPY fund over **6%**. At 2:45:28 the futures
exchange paused trading for **five seconds**; prices then stabilised and began to recover (CFTC–SEC report).

## The crisis spreads to individual shares

As prices plunged, many market makers' automated systems paused, and some firms "withdrew completely from the
markets". Sell orders, including "many retail stop-loss orders", met almost no buyers. In the 20 minutes from 2:40
p.m., **over 20,000 trades in more than 300 securities** were done at prices **60% or more** away from their 2:40 p.m.
prices, some at **a penny** and some at US\$100,000. Shares of the consulting firm **Accenture** "fell from nearly
\$40 to one cent and recovered all of their value within a matter of seconds" (CFTC–SEC report).

By about 3:00 p.m. most prices were back to normal, and the exchanges cancelled the trades that were 60% or more
away. On Yahoo Finance's daily data, the S&P 500 fell as low as 1065.79 during the day, **8.6%** below the previous
close, and closed down **3.2%**.

## What changed

Regulators introduced **circuit breakers for individual shares**, pausing trading for five minutes after a 10% move
in five minutes, and clearer rules on which trades get cancelled (CFTC–SEC report).

## Who was hurt

Investors whose market or stop-loss orders were filled at absurd prices in those minutes; trades more than 60% away
were cancelled, but those just inside that limit stood. Long-term holders who didn't trade lost nothing by the end
of the day beyond the ordinary fall.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Liquidity is not guaranteed,** even for the largest shares and ETFs.
- **Order type matters.** A market or stop-loss order guarantees a sale, not a price.
- **Not trading in a panic can be the cheapest choice.** Most prices were back within half an hour.
