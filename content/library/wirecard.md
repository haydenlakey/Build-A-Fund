## What happened

**Wirecard** was a German payments company whose rapid growth took it "all the way to the top" of the **DAX 30**, which it
joined in **September 2018**. On **22 June 2020** it announced that **€1.9 billion** it had claimed to hold in escrow
accounts in the Philippines "did not actually exist", and its auditor had not finished auditing its annual report. It
filed for insolvency on **25 June 2020** (European Securities and Markets Authority, ESMA).

## The warnings that came first

ESMA's review of how German supervisors handled the case lists years of warnings, which "were systematically rejected
by the company":

- From **30 January 2019** the *Financial Times* published allegations of forged and backdated contracts in Singapore
  and "round-trip" transactions. Later articles questioned the revenue from Wirecard's "third-party acquiring"
  partners, which, according to internal documents the FT reported on, contributed **95% of EBITDA** and over half
  of revenue for 2016.
- On **18 February 2019** Germany's financial regulator, **BaFin**, banned short selling of Wirecard shares for two
  months. In April 2019 it filed a criminal complaint against market participants and two journalists on suspicion
  of market manipulation.
- On **25 April 2019** Wirecard published its 2018 accounts with an **unqualified ("clean") audit opinion** from EY.
- In October 2019 the company appointed **KPMG** to run a special forensic investigation. When KPMG's report came out on
  **28 April 2020**, Wirecard said it had not found incriminating evidence; BaFin then opened a market-manipulation
  probe into that communication.
- On **18 June 2020**, after EY told BaFin about **falsified balance confirmations** for the €1.9 billion of trust
  accounts, BaFin notified the public prosecutor (ESMA).

## Who was hurt

Shareholders, including the many index funds and institutions that held Wirecard as a DAX 30 member. On Yahoo Finance's
data for its US quote, the share price fell from about **US\$114 on 17 June 2020** to **US\$1.60 on 26 June**, a loss of
about 99% in seven trading days, from a peak of about US\$228 in August 2018.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Index membership is not a quality check.** A DAX 30 company can turn out to be a fraud, and index funds hold it until
  it is removed.
- **Independent confirmation is the test.** Wirecard's missing money was supposed to sit with third parties; the fraud
  was exposed when the auditor found the confirmations were falsified.
- **Take specific allegations seriously.** In this case the newspaper's allegations proved well founded, while the
  regulator had first investigated the journalists and short sellers.
