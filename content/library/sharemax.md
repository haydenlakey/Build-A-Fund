## What happened

**Property syndication** let ordinary investors buy a share in a commercial property, such as a shopping centre, for a
regular income. **Sharemax** promoted a series of these schemes; about **33,000 investors** put about **R5 billion** into
them (Business Day, 2018). One of them, **The Villa** in Pretoria, was described as a R3.5 billion project and
attracted **R1.5 billion** of investor money before it was built (Daily Investor, 2024).

In **2010** the South African Reserve Bank determined that Sharemax's funding practices violated the Banks Act. A court
later summarised it: "Sharemax was receiving deposits from investors in contravention of the Bank Act", and the Reserve
Bank ordered it to stop receiving money and return what it had taken (Moonstone, 2022). Only registered banks may take
deposits from the public, because banks are regulated precisely so that depositors are protected. The schemes could not
repay, and they collapsed.

## The aftermath

- **The Villa** was **75% complete** when the company failed and "has stood abandoned for 15 years", caught in litigation
  (Daily Investor, 2024).
- A rescue company, **Nova Property Group**, took over the failed schemes (Daily Investor).
- A former Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services ombud likened some of the projects to a **Ponzi scheme**, and the
  Hawks investigated fraud and Ponzi allegations; Sharemax's attorney denied them. As of 2018, no key role-players had
  been prosecuted (Business Day; Daily Investor).
- Who caused the losses is still argued in the courts. In 2022 the Financial Services Tribunal found an adviser who had
  sold the investment was not legally liable, because he could not reasonably have foreseen the Reserve Bank's
  intervention "that caused the collapse" (Moonstone, 2022).

## Who was hurt

The tens of thousands of investors who had put their savings into the schemes.
Many turned to the ombud: in 2018 it had a backlog of **1,300** syndication cases (Business Day).

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Know what you actually own.** R1.5 billion of investors' money went into The Villa, a building that was never finished.
- **Unlisted means no price and no exit.** When the scheme failed there was no market to sell into, and investors have
  waited years for any recovery.
- **Regulation exists for a reason.** Deposit-taking is restricted to banks so that depositors' money is supervised.
  The app's "unlisted promise" pop-up flags unlisted holdings with unusually high expected returns.
