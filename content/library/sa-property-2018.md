## What happened

Listed property (real estate investment trusts, or REITs, that own shopping centres, offices and warehouses
and pay out most of their rental income) had been one of South Africa's best-performing asset classes in the
years before 2018. Then, according to Morningstar's Eugene Visagie:

- The asset class returned **−25.2% for the twelve months to December 2018**, its **worst calendar year since
  the index began in 1993**.
- Its three-year return to December 2018 was **−1.1% a year**, against **11.7% a year** for the three years to
  December 2017.

On Yahoo's price data the FTSE/JSE SA Listed Property index (J253) went from **694.7 at the end of 2017 to
478.2 at the end of 2018**, down 31% before dividends.

## Why

The fall was led by the **Resilient group** of related companies: Resilient, Greenbay, NEPI Rockcastle,
Fortress (A and B) and Sirius Real Estate. Before the collapse the group made up **over 40% of the South
African Property Index (SAPY)**. Morningstar's account lists:

- a **Viceroy report** on the Resilient group, and an ongoing investigation into the group and the
  **valuation methodology** it used;
- more recent research on **NEPI Rockcastle**;
- a potential takeover bid for the UK's **Intu Properties** falling through;
- **local economic concerns** on top of these company-specific issues.

Resilient's share price fell from 12,638.6c at the end of 2017 to 5,312.9c a year later (−58%).

## What the regulator found

The Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) investigated the group. It is important to report how that
ended:

- **November 2019:** the FSCA found that Resilient's accounts did need restating (over how it consolidated the
  Siyakha Trusts), but that Resilient and its directors **did not make false, misleading or deceptive
  statements**, and it closed that investigation.
- **November 2020:** a separate probe into whether insiders and connected parties had supported the Resilient
  share price between October 2017 and February 2018 found **no evidence of market abuse**. The FSCA said it
  would reopen the case if further evidence arose.

So the share price collapse was real, but the regulator did not find the wrongdoing that had been alleged.

## Who was hurt

Anyone holding SA listed property, including through property unit trusts and ETFs that track the property
index, and balanced funds with a property slice. Because the Resilient group was such a large part of the
index, an investor who bought "the whole sector" for diversification carried a large bet on one group.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Look through the index.** A sector fund is only as diversified as the weights inside it.
- **Related companies can behave like one position.** The Resilient group's companies fell together because
  the same worries applied to all of them.
- **A strong run is not a guarantee.** Property's good years before 2018 made it look safer than it was.
