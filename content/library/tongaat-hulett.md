## What happened

**Tongaat Hulett** was a sugar and land business with operations in South Africa, Zimbabwe and Mozambique. Its JSE
listing went back 74 years (BusinessTech).

In May 2019 the company said it would have to restate its accounts and expected the hit to its equity to be
**R3.5–4.5 billion**. Its shares were suspended from the JSE in June. When the restated results came out in
December 2019 the write-offs had grown to **R11.886 billion**, partly because of land expropriated in Zimbabwe
(about R4 billion) and a R3 billion tax asset that could no longer be counted. **Equity stood at R62 million,
against a reported R12 billion**, and the company said it would probably need to raise R4 billion from
shareholders (The Herald / Business Day).

## How the profits were inflated

The board commissioned a forensic investigation by PwC. Its summary of the findings, covering the 2013 to 2019
financial years, described practices including (BizNews):

- **Land sales booked too early,** in some cases with backdated agreements, or before zoning and subdivision
  approvals had been obtained.
- **Crops valued too highly:** the carrying values of cane roots and standing cane were overstated, for example by
  capitalising overheads and using optimistic estimates.
- **Running costs treated as investment:** maintenance and other operating costs were capitalised into plant and
  machinery, which raises profit today and moves the cost into future years.
- **Financing booked as sales:** in Zimbabwe, financing arrangements were accounted for as sugar sales, and raw
  sugar was priced as refined sugar.

The summary named at least ten former senior executives, including the former chief executive, as involved "to a
greater or lesser extent", and pointed to "a culture of deference and lack of challenge" at the company. The board
said it would pursue civil claims to recover bonuses, apply to have people declared delinquent directors, and
work with the police and the National Prosecuting Authority (BizNews).

## Business rescue and delisting

The company never recovered. Its shares were suspended again on **20 July 2022** after it missed the deadline for
its annual results, and it entered voluntary **business rescue on 27 October 2022**. On 12 October 2026 the JSE
announced that Tongaat Hulett will be removed from the exchange on **3 November 2026**. According to BusinessTech,
"no payment will be made" to shareholders, and no value is expected to reach them when the business rescue ends.

## Who was hurt

Shareholders, who expect to get nothing back. South African funds, including any index fund that held the share, carried the loss in proportion to their
holding.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Earnings depend on judgement.** When revenue is recognised, how assets are valued and which costs are
  capitalised are choices, and aggressive choices can flatter profits for years.
- **A first estimate of the damage may be far too low.** Tongaat's went from at most R4.5 billion to
  R11.9 billion in seven months.
- **Size and history don't make a share safe.** The app's "largest company" measure shows how much of your fund
  would go with any single failure.
