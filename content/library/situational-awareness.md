## What happened

**Situational Awareness** was a hedge fund started in **July 2024** by Leopold Aschenbrenner, then 25, who
had left OpenAI and written a 2024 essay arguing that more capable AI would drive huge demand for
**semiconductors, memory, data centres and power**. Early backers included Stripe's co-founders Patrick and
John Collison, former GitHub chief executive Nat Friedman and investor Daniel Gross.

The fund put that view into practice:

- **Long** (betting on a rise): AI-infrastructure shares. Its top disclosed positions at the end of March 2026
  included Nebius, SanDisk, Micron and CoreWeave; reports also name SK Hynix.
- **Short** (betting on a fall): software companies, including Adobe, on the idea that AI would eat into
  their business.
- **Leverage:** Quartz reported the fund used **as much as 400%** leverage.

It worked spectacularly at first. The *Financial Times* reported a **439% net return through 30 June 2026**
(from a 24 July investor letter), and the *Wall Street Journal* (as cited by Quartz) more than **1,000% since
inception**.

## The fall

In **July 2026** AI-related shares sold off hard. Nebius, SanDisk, Micron and CoreWeave **each fell more than
35% in the month**; the Nasdaq-100 fell about 10% and South Korea's Kospi (home of SK Hynix) lost roughly a
third, according to the FT as reported by CNBC. At the same time **the short bets on software went against
the fund**: Adobe rose.

That combination is what borrowed money cannot survive. As the fund's positions lost value, its **prime
brokers** (Bank of America, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan) made **margin calls**: demands for more collateral or
smaller positions. By **30 July** the fund had sold its public portfolio to **Citadel**, reportedly at below
market prices.

Reports of the fund's size differ, so here is the range:

- **Peak size:** Quartz and Crypto Briefing put assets at about **\$45 billion** at the start of July; CNBC,
  reporting the sale, called it a **"roughly \$20 billion to \$24 billion vehicle"**. The sources don't explain
  the difference.
- **The sale:** a public book of about **\$16 billion**, bought at a discount of **around 10%** (Crypto Briefing).
- **The loss:** about **67% in July** (Crypto Briefing); about **\$10 billion** left afterwards, mostly private
  holdings including a stake in Anthropic (Quartz, Crypto Briefing).

## Who was hurt

- **The fund's investors**, who had seen huge paper gains and lost about two-thirds of the fund's value in a
  single month.
- **Anyone else crowded into the same shares.** A forced seller of billions of rands' worth of AI shares
  pushes their prices down for everyone holding them.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **One theme is one bet,** however many shares carry it. Micron, CoreWeave, SanDisk and Nebius are
  different companies, but in July 2026 they fell together.
- **A hedge only helps if it pays when the main bet loses.** Shorting software was meant to reduce risk; in
  the sell-off it lost money too.
- **Leverage takes away time.** Without borrowing, an investor convinced the thesis is right can wait. With
  400% leverage, the lender decides when to sell, and it is usually at the worst moment.
- **Big past returns describe the risk taken, not only the skill.** A fund up more than 400% in two years is
  very likely carrying risks that can work in reverse just as quickly.
