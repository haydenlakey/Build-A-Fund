## "Bogle's folly"

On **31 August 1976** Vanguard, the firm founded by John Bogle, launched **First Index Investment Trust**, the fund now
called the Vanguard 500 Index Fund. Instead of paying managers to pick shares, it simply held the shares in the S&P 500
index. Bogle hoped to raise **US\$50–150 million**; the offering raised "a little more than \$11 million", and he later
called it "an abject failure". Its annual costs at launch were **0.43%** of assets; today its cheapest share classes charge
**0.03–0.04%** (Vanguard).

On Yahoo Finance's data (chart), a dollar in the fund at the end of 1980 had grown about **123 times** by October 2026,
with dividends reinvested, about **11% a year**.

## Why indexing works: the arithmetic

Before costs, the average rand invested actively in a market must earn the market's return, because all the active
investors together *are* the market. After costs, the average active rand must therefore earn **less** than the market,
by roughly the extra fees and trading costs. An index fund earns the market's return minus a much smaller fee. Some
active funds beat the index, but they have to overcome that cost gap first.

In 2024 the average US index fund charged **0.11%** a year against **0.59%** for active funds (Vanguard).

## The South African evidence

S&P Dow Jones Indices publishes **SPIVA** scorecards comparing active funds with their benchmark indices. Its first South
African scorecard, in 2015, found that (Africa Global Funds):

- **83.69%** of SA equity funds underperformed the S&P South Africa DSW index over one year, and about **84.68%** over five
  years;
- over five years, **96%** of global equity funds underperformed the S&P Global 1200; and
- about **20–30%** of funds were liquidated or merged over five years, so a fund's past record may not even exist by the
  time you want to judge it.

Short-term bond funds were an exception: active managers there did beat their benchmark (Africa Global Funds).

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Costs compound.** A fee difference of half a percent a year adds up over decades (see the Fees article).
- **The index is the benchmark to beat.** The app's "against a benchmark" page shows whether a fund's extra risk is
  being paid for.
- **Survivorship flatters active funds.** Funds that do badly tend to close, so the ones still around look better than
  the whole group did.
