## What happened

**Steinhoff International** was a global retail group listed on the JSE. It owned or held large stakes in retailers across Europe, Africa and the US, including **Pepkor** (Pep, Ackermans,
Incredible Connection), **Pepco** in Europe, **Conforama** in France and **Mattress Firm** in the US.

On **5 December 2017** the chief executive, **Markus Jooste**, resigned "in connection to the accounting
irregularities" that the auditor, Deloitte, had pushed the company to investigate during its 2017 audit.

## How far the shares fell

- **58% on the first day** of trading on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange after the resignation.
- **Almost 90% within two weeks.**
- Still **down 96%** in March 2019, a loss of about **$15 billion** of market value.

Losses to investors overall have been put at more than **R250 billion**.

## Why

A forensic investigation by **PwC** found that between **2009 and 2017** fictitious and irregular transactions
had inflated income by **over €6.5 billion** (about $7.4 billion). Profits that investors had relied on for
years had not been earned.

## Who was hurt

- **Shareholders**, directly or through the funds that held the share.
- By early 2021 Steinhoff faced **over 90 lawsuits** in South Africa, Germany and the Netherlands. A settlement
  with a pot of around **R25 billion** was set up to compensate claimants, and Deloitte agreed to contribute
  **up to R1.3 billion**. The R25 billion pot is about a tenth of the R250 billion estimate of investors' losses.
- The company was **delisted in October 2023**.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Size and fame are not safety.** Steinhoff was big and international. A share can lose
  almost all its value within weeks when the accounts turn out to be false.
- **Concentration turns one fraud into a disaster.** A holding that is 2% of a fund costs that fund about 2% if
  it collapses; one that is 30% costs about 30%.
- **Governance red flags are part of due diligence:** a dominant chief executive, many acquisitions, complex
  cross-border structures and profits that are hard to trace to cash. None proves fraud, but they make it
  harder to find.
