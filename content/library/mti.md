## What happened

**Mirror Trading International** was a South African company run by **Johann Steynberg**. From about
**May 2018** it took bitcoin from the public, saying a **proprietary trading "bot"** would trade foreign
exchange with it, and attracted investors with claims of **at least 10% a month**. The CFTC later described it as
an **"international fraudulent multilevel marketing scheme"**.

South Africa's Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA) said, in a circular reported at the start of 2021, that MTI and its senior management were
**"conducting an illegal operation"**. A Cape Town court granted a provisional liquidation order, and by the
start of 2021 Steynberg's whereabouts were unknown. Finance Magnates reported that MTI was believed to hold
about **R9.45 billion** for **almost 28,000 investors**.

## What the regulators found

The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) took MTI and Steynberg to court. In **April 2023** a federal
court found that:

- Between about May 2018 and March 2021, MTI took **at least 29,421 bitcoin**, worth **over $1.73 billion** at
  the end of March 2021, from at least **23,000 people in the US** and many more worldwide.
- The trading bot claim was **false**, and **all** of the bitcoin was misappropriated.
- Steynberg was ordered to pay **$1.73 billion in restitution** to victims and a civil penalty of the same size,
  the **largest penalty in any CFTC case**. He had been **detained in Brazil since December 2021** on an
  INTERPOL warrant.

## Why it worked for as long as it did

- **The promise was huge and specific.** 10% a month compounds to more than **214% a year** (1.1¹² ≈ 3.14).
- **There was no trading to produce returns.** When the claimed source of profit doesn't exist, any money paid
  out can only come from investors' own deposits, so a scheme like this lasts only as long as new money keeps
  arriving.
- **It spread through people.** Multi-level marketing means investors bring in other investors.

## Who was hurt

Tens of thousands of ordinary people in South Africa and abroad: at least 23,000 in the US alone, by the CFTC's
count, and almost 28,000 investors in total by Finance Magnates' earlier estimate. The two counts come from different
sources and dates, so they don't add up neatly.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Compare any promise with what markets actually pay.** The chart shows R1 000 at 10% a month against the JSE
  Top 40 (through Satrix 40) over the same years. Real investments go up and down; a smooth, enormous return is
  the red flag.
- **Check the licence.** In South Africa the FSCA keeps a register of authorised financial services providers.
- **"Bot", "algorithm" and "crypto" are not explanations** of where a return comes from. If you can't say who
  pays you and why, you are not able to judge the risk.
