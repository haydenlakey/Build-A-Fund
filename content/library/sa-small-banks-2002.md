## What happened

**Saambou Bank** was a South African bank with **520,000 depositors**. In early 2002 it suffered "a marked loss of
confidence in the bank and its management" and an "abnormal outflow of funds" that put it under liquidity pressure. At
the close of business on **Saturday 9 February 2002**, the Minister of Finance, Trevor Manuel, placed it under
**curatorship** under section 69 of the Banks Act (Minister of Finance, statement to Parliament, 14 February 2002).

Saambou wasn't alone that year. **Regal Treasury Bank** failed after its auditors withdrew their approval of its
controlling company's financial statements, causing a run; **New Republic Bank** failed with management and liquidity
problems and was merged into another bank (BusinessTech list of failed banks).

## Why the government didn't bail it out

The Minister's statement set out the choice:

- Giving Saambou government money "would not be prudent", because there was no guarantee it would restore confidence
  rather than "fund further large net outflows".
- Government's responsibility was "protecting the interests of depositors – the widows, workers and small businesses and
  not to bail out shareholders, who in any event assume the risk of their investment".
- Curatorship meant **freezing all accounts** while the curator took stock. Depositors with balances of R4,000 or less
  (292,000 of the 520,000) could withdraw in full, and the curator worked on access for salaries and pensions paid in
  after 9 February.

He also stressed that the system as a whole was sound: South Africa had **41 registered banks**, and a joint IMF and
World Bank assessment had found the regulatory environment sound.

## What the share market showed

On the app's data (chart), the Top 40 index rose in the six months to March 2002 while the big four banks lagged: Absa
was about **30% lower** at the end of March 2002 than in early October 2001, and FirstRand and Nedcor were down about
13–17% (dividends reinvested). In April they partly caught up.

## Who was hurt

Saambou's shareholders, and depositors whose money was frozen while the curator worked, especially those relying on
interest from their deposits.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Runs are about confidence as much as solvency.** The Registrar said there was "no reason to believe that SAAMBOU is
  insolvent", yet it still had to be closed to stop the outflow.
- **Deposits can be frozen.** Cash in a bank under curatorship may be out of reach for a while.
- **Shareholders are not protected the way depositors are.** The government's stated priority was depositors, not
  shareholders, "who in any event assume the risk of their investment".
