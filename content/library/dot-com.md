## What happened

In the second half of the 1990s the internet went from a curiosity to something everyone could see
would change the world. Investors poured money into anything connected to it. The technology-heavy
**Nasdaq Composite** index closed at a record **5 048.62 on 10 March 2000**, up from about 744 at the
start of 1995 in Yahoo's data.

Then it fell. By **9 October 2002** the Nasdaq had closed at **1 114.11**, about **78% below its peak**,
giving back the whole bubble. It didn't close above its March 2000 record again until **23 April 2015**,
fifteen years later.

## Why

The five phases played out almost exactly:

- **Displacement:** the internet and the commercial web were genuinely transformative.
- **Euphoria and mania:** companies could list on the stock exchange without ever having made a profit,
  and investors were eager to buy "at any valuation". The Wikipedia account notes the Nasdaq's
  price-to-earnings ratio reached around 200.
- **Distress:** the U.S. Federal Reserve raised interest rates several times, which many believe helped
  burst the bubble, and the cash many young companies were burning through became harder to replace.
- **Revulsion:** funding dried up and the failures began.

## Who was hurt

Pets.com went out of business in November 2000, about nine months after listing. Webvan, Boo.com and
many others shut down. Even companies that survived and kept growing fell hard: Cisco, a maker of the
internet's actual plumbing, lost about 80% of its share price. By the end of the downturn in 2002 U.S.
shares had lost about **$5 trillion** of market value from the peak.

People who had concentrated their savings in technology shares, often because those shares had done so
well, suffered the most. Many sold near the bottom.

## The lesson for a fund builder

The internet did change the world, and some companies from that era became the largest on earth. The
investors who lost were not wrong about the technology. They paid prices that assumed the best case
for almost every company at once, and they held little else. A fund heavy in one sector depends on one
story; a diversified fund can survive that story going badly for a decade.
