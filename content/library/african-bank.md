## What happened

African Bank Investments (Abil) was a major provider of **unsecured loans**: loans with nothing pledged
as security, typically at high interest rates to borrowers who could not offer collateral.

On **Sunday 10 August 2014**, with effect from 16:00, the Minister of Finance and the Registrar of Banks
placed African Bank under **curatorship**: an administrator (the curator) took control from the board.
The South African Reserve Bank (SARB) said the bank's problems were "largely specific to their current
business model". Trading in the shares and in the group's bonds was suspended the next morning.

## The rescue plan

The SARB split the bank in two:

- A **"bad bank"** of non-performing and high-risk loans, with a book value of R17 billion net of
  impairments, which the SARB bought for **R7 billion**.
- A **"good bank"** with a book value of R26 billion, to be recapitalised with **R10 billion** of new
  capital underwritten by a consortium of the big banks.

Retail depositors were moved across at full value. **Senior bondholders and wholesale depositors were
moved across at 90% of face value**: a 10% loss on lending that many had considered very safe. Existing
shareholders and subordinated-debt holders were only offered the chance to take part in the new
capital raising.

## Who was hurt

- **Shareholders**, whose shares were suspended and who were left only with the option to take part in
  the new capital raising.
- **Bondholders and wholesale depositors**, which took the 10% haircut. They included money-market funds,
  which many savers treat as cash: Absa's money-market fund had about 3% of its assets in African Bank and
  marked its clients' money down by about 0.3%.
- **Other unsecured lenders' shares** fell on the news, as the chart shows for Capitec (down about 9% by mid-August), although their
  own banks were not placed under curatorship.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Fast growth in lending is a warning as much as a strength.** The profits show up immediately; the
  defaults arrive later.
- **"Safe" income can carry real losses.** Bonds and bank deposits are only as safe as the borrower.
- **A sector shock spreads.** If several of your holdings share one business model (here, lending to
  stretched consumers), one failure can hit them all at once.
