## What happened

On **24 February 2022**, in response to "Russia's further invasion of Ukraine", the US Treasury announced what it called
"unprecedented and expansive" sanctions. They targeted banks holding **nearly 80% of all banking assets in Russia**,
including full blocking sanctions on VTB, Russia's second-largest financial institution, and barred US persons from
dealing in new debt or equity of major Russian state-owned companies and banks (US Treasury).

On Yahoo Finance's quotes the rouble went from about **76 to the dollar** in mid-February to about **139 on 8 March**
(chart).

## The index provider's decision

On 28 February, the index company **MSCI** asked international investors whether the Russian market could still be
invested in. "An overwhelming majority" said it was **"currently uninvestable"**. On **2 March** MSCI announced it would
remove Russian shares from its emerging-markets indices, "at a price that is effectively zero", as of the close on
**9 March 2022** (MSCI).

That matters for anyone holding an emerging-markets index fund: the index those funds track now counted Russian shares
at effectively nothing.

## And then?

The rouble's quoted rate recovered: by late June it was around **54 to the dollar**, stronger than before the war
(chart). But a recovering exchange rate inside Russia did little for a foreign fund that could not trade its Russian
shares or bring the money home.

## Who was hurt

Foreign investors in Russian shares and bonds, including ordinary savers who held them through emerging-markets funds;
and, far more seriously, the people of Ukraine, which is the context of every figure here.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Country risk includes politics and sanctions,** not just economics and currency.
- **Diversification limits the damage.** A broad emerging-markets fund lost only its Russian slice; a fund concentrated
  in one country can lose most of its value this way.
- **"Investable" is a condition, not a given.** Market access, capital controls and sanctions decide whether a price on
  a screen is one you can actually trade at.
