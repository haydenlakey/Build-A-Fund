## What happened

In early 2020 the world already had more oil than it needed. Then Covid-19 caused "an unprecedented reduction in
demand". Traders worried whether producers could cut output fast enough, and above all whether there would be anywhere
to store the surplus, especially at **Cushing, Oklahoma**, the delivery point for the main US crude contract. By March
2020 the working storage available at Cushing was near capacity (CFTC staff report).

The **WTI** futures contract is **physically settled**: whoever holds a contract when it expires must take delivery of
real barrels at Cushing. On **Monday 20 April 2020**, the day before the May contract expired, its price fell from
**US\$17.73** at the start of the session to settle at **minus US\$37.63 a barrel**, after touching **minus US\$40.32**.
It was the first time the contract had traded below zero since it was listed in 1983. All the later delivery months
settled at positive prices that day (CFTC).

A negative price means sellers paid buyers to take the oil: holders of expiring contracts who couldn't take delivery
had to pay someone else to.

## Why it matters beyond oil traders

Brent crude, the international benchmark, fell too but stayed positive (chart). On Yahoo Finance's data, both
benchmarks were between about two-thirds and four-fifths below their early-January levels in late April, and recovered to around US\$40 by the end of June.

Many funds that give investors exposure to oil do it by holding futures and "rolling" them into later months before
expiry. Their returns depend on the futures prices of the months they hold, which can differ a lot from the spot price
of oil, especially when storage is scarce.

## Who was hurt

Anyone still holding the May WTI contract on 20 April without a way to store oil, and investors in products that held
nearby WTI futures. Oil producers, whose oil was suddenly worth very little.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Know what a commodity fund actually holds.** A fund of futures contracts is not a barrel of oil in a tank.
- **"It can't go below zero" is not a law.** When storage or delivery breaks down, prices can.
- **Commodities and commodity shares are cyclical and can move violently,** as the 2014–16 slump also showed.
