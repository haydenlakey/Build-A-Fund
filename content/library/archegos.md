## What happened

**Archegos Capital Management** was the family office of the investor Bill Hwang. According to the US Securities and Exchange Commission's complaint
(these are allegations as filed in April 2022):

- Archegos grew from about **US\$4 billion** of assets to **over US\$36 billion** in just under six months.
- On 1 January 2021 it had about US\$7.7 billion of capital and US\$54 billion of gross exposure; by **22 March
  2021**, over **US\$36 billion of capital and over US\$160 billion of gross exposure**, more than four times its
  capital.
- Most of the exposure came through **security-based swaps** with about a dozen banks: contracts in which a bank
  pays Archegos the gains on a share (or charges it the losses), in exchange for fees and collateral. The SEC
  alleges Archegos misled its banks about the size, concentration and liquidity of its positions.
- Its portfolio moved away from giants like Amazon and Microsoft towards less liquid shares: Chinese companies such
  as Baidu, Tencent Music and iQIYI, and US media companies ViacomCBS and Discovery. By late March 2021, by its own
  estimates, its exposure equalled **over 70%** of the shares of GSX Techedu, over 60% of Discovery's class A shares
  and over 50% of ViacomCBS and iQIYI.

The SEC alleges that Hwang used this buying power to push prices up. ViacomCBS's share price rose from around US\$40
in early January 2021 to US\$100 on 22 March, "approximately 150% in less than three months", and on being asked in
June 2020 whether a rise in its price was "a sign of strength", Hwang replied: "No. It is a sign of me buying."

## The collapse

In the week of 22 March 2021, falls in Archegos's largest positions triggered **margin calls** it could not meet.
After the market closed on 25 March it tried to raise cash through a block sale, and its banks then sent default
notices and sold the positions (SEC complaint). On Yahoo Finance's closes, Tencent Music fell **34%** and iQIYI
**41%** between 22 and 31 March. ViacomCBS closed at US\$41.71 on 23 April (SEC complaint).

The banks that had lent to Archegos were left with the losses. An independent report for **Credit Suisse** found
the default "on March 25, 2021" cost the bank **US\$5.5 billion**, and blamed "a fundamental failure of management
and controls" in its investment bank, whose senior managers "persistently failed to address risks connected with
trades made by Archegos"; it found no evidence of fraud or illegal conduct by the bank's staff (Paul, Weiss).

## Who was hurt

Archegos collapsed. Its lending banks, above all Credit Suisse, took billions in credit losses.
And other shareholders in the companies Archegos had piled into bought at prices that, the SEC alleges, had been
pushed up artificially.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Concentration plus leverage is how investors get wiped out.** Four times leverage on a handful of illiquid
  shares means a 25% fall in those shares can erase the whole capital.
- **Each lender saw only part of the picture.** With about a dozen banks each holding some of the swaps, the full
  size of Archegos's bets became clear only when they were being sold.
- **A price pushed up by one buyer falls when that buyer stops.** A share's recent rise says little about its value
  if the buying behind it is about to reverse.
