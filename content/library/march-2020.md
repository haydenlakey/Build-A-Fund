## What happened

On **11 March 2020** the World Health Organization's Director-General said: "We have therefore made the
assessment that COVID-19 can be characterized as a pandemic." There were by then more than **118,000 cases in
114 countries**, and **4,291 people** had died.

Markets had already started falling in late February. In the app's weekly data, from **14 February to
20 March 2020**:

| | Change |
|---|---|
| FTSE/JSE Top 40 index | **−30.3%** |
| S&P 500 (SPY) in rand | **−20.7%** |
| Long US Treasuries (TLT) in rand | **+28.5%** |
| NewGold gold ETF (in rand) | **+11.1%** |
| US dollar in rand | **+16.4%** (R14.90 → R17.34) |

The rand kept weakening after that: the dollar peaked at about **R19.10** in the week to **24 April 2020** in
the app's weekly data.

## Why the offshore assets cushioned the fall

Two things happened together. Global shares fell, but **less in rand than in dollars**, because the rand fell
at the same time: an investor in rand owning US shares lost on the shares and gained on the currency. And
investors everywhere rushed to the assets they treat as safest, **US government bonds and gold**, whose prices
rose; in rand they rose even more.

## The recovery

The fall was fast, and so was the rebound. By the end of June 2020 the Top 40 was back to about 95% of its
mid-February level and the S&P 500 in rand was above it. An investor who sold at the bottom in March locked in
the loss and missed that rebound.

## Who was hurt

- **Anyone who had to sell** in March, for example to raise cash, and so turned a temporary fall into a
  permanent loss.
- **Funds with only South African shares**, which took the full fall with no currency cushion.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Diversification that works in a crisis is about behaviour, not labels.** Many JSE shares fell together; the
  holdings that helped were priced in dollars or treated as havens.
- **The rand is part of your risk.** For a South African, offshore assets add currency risk, but in March 2020
  (as in December 2015, see Nenegate) the rand fell in the panic, which cushioned them.
- **Shocks are short-term risk.** The chart's rebound is one episode, not a promise; the money you might need
  soon is the money a shock can hurt most.
