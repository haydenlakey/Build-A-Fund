## What happened in 2016

UK retail investors could buy **open-ended property funds** that owned offices, shops and warehouses directly and let
investors buy or sell units every day. After the result of the **EU referendum in June 2016**, so many investors
asked for their money back that "dealing in a number of property funds was temporarily suspended, preventing
investors from accessing their money". Dealing in all of them "had resumed by the end of the year" (Financial Conduct
Authority, FCA).

Listed property shares, which trade on the stock exchange, kept trading and showed the hit instead. On Yahoo Finance's
data an ETF of UK listed property companies fell about **22%** in the two trading days after the vote, while the FTSE 100
was down about 6% (chart).

## What happened in 2020

In **March 2020**, as Covid-19 spread, the valuers who value these funds' buildings said there was **"material
valuation uncertainty"**: buildings could still be valued, but much less reliably. Under rules the FCA had set in 2019,
due to take effect in September 2020, a property fund with **more than 20%** of its portfolio subject to material
valuation uncertainty must suspend dealing, and existing rules already required managers to consider it (Association
of Real Estate Funds, 18 March 2020). A number of property funds suspended again.

## Why suspension happens

A fund that holds buildings can't sell one in a day; a sale takes months. If many investors leave at once, the fund
either sells buildings quickly at a discount, which hurts those who stay, or stops redemptions until it can sell in
an orderly way. The FCA put it like this: without suspension it "would have been very difficult to ensure investors
remaining in the funds were treated equally and fairly", and the 2016 suspensions "generally worked as they were
intended to" and prevented wider market disruption.

The FCA also noted the **liquidity mismatch**: these funds can hold up to 100% of their assets in property while
offering daily dealing. It linked the issue to the 2019 suspension of the **Woodford Equity Income Fund**, which held
hard-to-sell unlisted shares (FCA, PS19/24).

## Who was hurt

Investors who needed their money while the funds were suspended.
In 2016, investors who sold listed property shares at the low on 27 June sold about 22% below the pre-vote price; by
the end of August the ETF had recovered to about 7% below it (chart).

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Check what the fund owns, not just how often it deals.** Daily dealing on illiquid assets is a promise that can be
  suspended.
- **The app treats unlisted holdings as illiquid** and flags a fund when a large share can't be sold in a hurry.
- **Listed and unlisted versions of the same asset behave differently.** A listed property share shows the fall at
  once; an unlisted fund may show it later, or stop you leaving.
