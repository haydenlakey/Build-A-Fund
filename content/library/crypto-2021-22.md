## What happened

Crypto assets boomed in 2021. On Yahoo Finance's daily prices, **Bitcoin** rose from about US\$29,000 at the end of
2020 to a peak of about **US\$67,600 on 8 November 2021**, and **Ether** rose about **6.5 times**, from about US\$740 to
about US\$4,800 on the same day.

Then came the fall, with two collapses inside the industry along the way:

- **Bitcoin fell 77%** to about US\$15,800 on 21 November 2022, and **Ether 79%** to about US\$990 in June 2022.
- Over a similar period the tech-heavy **Nasdaq-100** fund QQQ fell **35%**, from December 2021 to November 2022: a
  painful fall, but far smaller.

## TerraUSD: the "stable" coin that wasn't

**TerraUSD (UST)** was an "algorithmic stablecoin", meant to stay worth one US dollar through the workings of its
sister token **LUNA** rather than by holding dollars in reserve. It was marketed as "yield-bearing", paying "as much as
20 percent interest" through a platform called Anchor. In **May 2022** UST lost its link to the dollar, and its price
and that of its sister tokens fell "to close to zero". The US Securities and Exchange Commission later charged its
issuer, Terraform Labs, and its chief executive Do Kwon with "orchestrating a multi-billion dollar crypto asset
securities fraud" (SEC, February 2023).

## FTX: the exchange that lent out its customers' money

**FTX**, a large crypto exchange based in the Bahamas, had raised more than **US\$1.8 billion** from equity investors
(SEC). According to the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission's complaint, customer assets were "routinely
accepted and held by Alameda" (a trading firm also controlled by FTX's founder, Sam Bankman-Fried) "and commingled
with Alameda's funds", and were used for real estate, political contributions and "high-risk, illiquid digital asset
industry investments". The CFTC says the conduct ran until **11 November 2022** and caused "the loss of over \$8
billion in FTX customer deposits". These are the regulators' allegations as filed in December 2022.

## Why it fell so far

Shares and bonds pay (or are expected to pay) cash to their owners, which puts some anchor under their value. Most
crypto assets pay nothing, so their price depends entirely on what other people will pay for them later. When
enthusiasm reversed in 2022, there was little to stop the fall. The two collapses above made it worse: a coin sold as
stable and paying 20% went to almost nothing, and an exchange allegedly used its customers' deposits for its own
bets.

## Who was hurt

People who bought near the 2021 peak, holders of UST who believed it was as good as a dollar, and FTX customers who
couldn't get their deposits back.

## The lesson for a fund builder

- **Volatility this large dominates a fund.** A small slice of an asset that can fall 77% in a year can contribute
  a large share of a fund's risk; the app's risk contribution measure shows this.
- **"Stable" and "yield" are claims, not guarantees.** Ask what backs them and who holds the assets.
- **A platform is a counterparty.** Assets held on an exchange are only as safe as the exchange.
